## The rules you both scored by

## How to score a memo

Five criteria, each scored 0, 1 or 2, for a total out of 10. On the five control plans criterion C2 is not scored: the other four are added up and multiplied by 10/8.

Before scoring, the harness cuts every memo after its 400th word and records where it cut. Length and style earn nothing.

For each criterion, check the anchors from 0 upwards and give the first that fits.

C1: Finds the plan's own problems
Reads: the key's three problems
0: Finds none of the three problems on the key.
1: Finds one of them.
2: Finds two or all three.
Note: A problem counts when the memo names it and says why it matters, in any words. Other problems, true or not, earn nothing here.

C2: Uses what the organisation said at earlier meetings
Reads: the key's earlier facts, each with its implication and must_state details, and the key's unsound options
0: Recommends an option the key marks unsound, even with conditions; or neither uses a listed fact nor names the specific question that would uncover one.
1: Uses at least one listed fact correctly; or names the specific question for a listed fact (for example "check how long the lease runs") and makes the recommendation depend on the answer.
2: Uses every listed fact correctly and recommends exactly one option the key does not mark unsound.
Note: A fact is used only when the memo states every detail in its must_state list, in any words, and applies it to the decision. Earlier facts the key does not list, used correctly, are neither rewarded nor penalised. Not scored on control plans.

C3: Recommends a clear decision
0: Does not pick exactly one of the lettered options.
1: Picks one option but gives no reason, or no condition that would change it.
2: Picks one option, gives the main reason, and says what would change it.

C4: Weighs the options against the organisation's situation
0: Considers only the recommended option.
1: Mentions the other options without comparing them all.
2: Compares every option on cost, time, people or risk, using the request or anything the organisation said at earlier meetings.

C5: Stays accurate
0: Contains a material error, or states as fact something that none of the operator messages in the packet said, such as an invented number, decision or constraint.
1: Contains a minor error that does not change the recommendation.
2: Contains no errors.

## How to mark a recall reply

An answer is right when it gives every item in the key's must_include list, in any words, and says nothing that contradicts the key. "We don't know" is wrong. An answer that mixes the right answer with a wrong one is wrong.

## The combined list

C1: C5 scores 0 for an invented fact only when the memo presents as established something about the organisation, its plan or its numbers that no operator message said; a figure plainly marked as the memo's own estimate ("roughly", "likely", "estimated") or as an if-then scenario is not stated as fact and does not by itself trigger 0.
C2: A marked estimate or scenario is still an error when it contradicts a packet figure or its arithmetic does not follow from packet figures; score 1 if the recommendation stands without it and 0 if the recommendation rests on it.
C3: General reasoning about how audiences, sponsors or suppliers usually behave is argument, not an invented fact about the organisation; lower C5 for it only if it is plainly false or contradicts the packet.
C4: Give C5 a 1 only when you can quote the specific statement that is wrong and say what the packet or the arithmetic gives instead; material that merely sounds unsupported, with no such statement, scores 2.
C5: Describing what a plan implicitly relies on is critique, not error; it becomes a C5 error only when the memo attributes to the plan or the operator a number, decision or constraint the packet does not contain.
C6: C4 scores 2 only when every lettered option, including each rejected one, is weighed on at least one of cost, time, people or risk with a reason drawn from the request or earlier meetings; one dimension per option is enough, and money earned or saved counts as cost.
C7: A bare verdict on an option ("not viable", "not a substitute", "useful alongside") is a mention, not a comparison; because anchors are checked from 0 upwards, a memo that weighs some options and only labels another fits the 1 anchor first.
C8: Saying what would make an option acceptable or more useful is a condition, which counts toward C3, not a weighing of that option for C4.
C9: Weighing counts for C4 wherever it appears in the memo before the cut, including the critique section, not only under an options heading.
C10: C4 asks whether the options were weighed, not whether the weighing is correct; a wrong figure or claim inside the comparison is scored once, under C5.
C11: C5, anchor 0: "states as fact" means a flat, unhedged assertion about this organisation (a number, decision, constraint or event) that no operator message contains; one such assertion gives 0 however small it looks, because anchors are checked from 0 upwards.
C12: C5: A figure the memo clearly marks as its own estimate or projection ("roughly", "likely", "an estimated") is not stated as fact and is not by itself an error; it counts against the memo only if it contradicts figures in the packet.
C13: C5: A number correctly derived from the packet's own numbers is not invented; check the arithmetic, and treat a wrong result as a material error if the recommendation or a comparison rests on it and a minor error if it does not.
C14: C5: General claims about how audiences, sponsors, suppliers or technology usually behave are reasoning, not invented facts, unless they give a specific unsourced figure or assert something about this particular organisation as known.
C15: C5: Proposed conditions, questions to check, extra costs flagged as possibilities, and the memo's reading of what a plan implicitly assumes are judgement, not factual claims; they are errors only where they misstate what an operator message actually said.
C16: C5: Give 1 only when you can point to a specific statement that is wrong against the packet or wrong in arithmetic and the recommendation would stand without it; if you cannot quote such a statement, give 2, and do not use 1 to register doubt about hedged estimates or plausible but unlisted risks.
