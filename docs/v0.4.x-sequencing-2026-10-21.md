## Amendment 2026-10-21 — the goal replaces the product reading, and the licence default applies

The first strategy review was held with the [v0.4.0 plan](v0.4.0-plan.md)'s opening and logged in [strategy §10](project-strategy.md#10-review-cadence-and-log) on 2026-10-20. It made two rulings and applied one more of [§4](project-strategy.md#4-the-decision-framework)'s defaults. §10 says a review that changes a decision is ratified only by the sequencing amendment that acts on it, so this amendment ratifies all three. It also settles what they change: the rules the product reading set, the licence, and the strategy document. It adds no capability and changes no version's scope.

### Why this changes

1. **The review has ruled, and only an amendment ratifies it.** On 2026-10-20 the maintainer chose two things. The goal of ruling (g) replaces the product reading chosen on 2026-09-12. §4's licence default applies, since no commercial conversation is in progress. The review also applied §4's other default and froze the workflow engine, which EXP-001 does not use.
2. **Ruling (g) kept three things binding until now.** It held the External evidence rule, the README rule in ruling (j) and §4's defaults in force until the first review ruled. That review has ruled, so each one is now kept, replaced or applied, by name. Left alone, the product reading would keep deciding through the rules it set, after the review that withdrew it.
3. **Rule 6, as read so far, blocks the goal.** Both amendments of 2026-10-18 read rule 6 as letting them add no new capability while the section is empty, because under the product reading only outside use could justify building more. Read that way, nothing the v0.4.0 plan's goal audit proposes can board while the section stays empty. That covers RFC 0021 Phase 3's reminders, ISSUE-0186's memory bound, ISSUE-0161's stalled turn, ISSUE-0157's shared budget, ISSUE-0156's unbounded timer and RFC 0027's reflection. Under the goal the society is what is being built, and experiments measure whether it works, EXP-002 above all. The failure the rule guards against is a release scoped on internal correctness alone, and that is just as likely under the goal. So the bar stays, and only what clears it changes.
4. **The README waits for a choice that no longer exists.** Ruling (j) holds the README until EXP-001 reports because, under the product reading, its pitch was to be the wedge EXP-001's rule chose. Ruling (h) took the pitch from that rule and gave it to the goal. Nothing the README would say still waits on EXP-001, except what it claims has been measured.
5. **A licence change is a PR of its own.** It touches every file that states the licence and needs [RFC 0045](rfcs/0045-open-core-extraction-policy.md)'s provenance check. What it publishes under Apache-2.0 cannot be withdrawn. So this amendment makes the decision and names the PR.

### Rulings

Rulings (a) to (l) live in [v0.3.x-sequencing.md](v0.3.x-sequencing.md) and (m) to (s) in [v0.4.x-sequencing.md](v0.4.x-sequencing.md#rulings); each holds as last amended. Each ruling below holds until a later amendment says otherwise.

- **(t)** The goal replaces the product reading. The review's first ruling is ratified. The reading the maintainer chose under [strategy §4](project-strategy.md#4-the-decision-framework) on 2026-09-12, a product meant to find users, no longer decides anything. The goal of ruling (g) does. Rulings (u) to (x) settle what that reading set, so the clause of ruling (g) that kept it binding has done its work. §4's decision rules keep the meaning ruling (h) gave them, and [§5](project-strategy.md#5-direction-the-working-hypothesis)'s working sentence stays only the hypothesis for the plan-critique job. The strategy document is rewritten around the goal by a PR of its own, not by this amendment. Until that PR lands, this amendment holds wherever the two disagree. The first review ordered no governance-core extraction, and no wedge decision will come, so three things left to it pass to the next scheduled strategy review: that extraction, and with it [RFC 0046](rfcs/0046-budget-lease-extraction.md) (ruling c); the fate of the components a wedge would not have needed; and whether and when the Rust CLI and the workflow engine are removed.
- **(u)** Rule 6 stays, read for the goal. Every amendment still opens with the External evidence section, with the same rows, and `make amendment-evidence-check` does not change. The bar stays too: an amendment whose section holds no positive entry may not scope a version on internal correctness alone. Until now it was cleared by a positive entry in the section (an outside ask, say) or by a dependency that a scoped phase states in its own plan (ruling n). Under the goal, an addition also clears it when all three of these hold:
  - a goal audit proposes it, in a version plan or in the amendment itself;
  - the amendment says how it serves the goal (ruling g);
  - the amendment names the experiment whose result bears on it, EXP-002 or a later one, and that experiment's merged pre-registration names the comparison that isolates the addition and the outcome that counts against it.

  An addition argued from the goal that misses any of the three does not board while the section is empty. One that boards this way carries that outcome as its cut clause. Every scheduled strategy review lists the additions boarded this way, beside their experiments' results once published, and may tighten this route. Other work justified by internal correctness alone, such as a sweep or a refactor, still does not board. Scope an earlier amendment placed or offered, such as v0.4.0's cut items offered to v0.4.1, carries over without clearing the bar again. An issue boarded this way also needs its amendment to narrow ruling (q), since no boarded phase fixes it. The goal route does not clear ruling (b): memory isolation, attribution and audience work still needs an outside ask, ISSUE-0186's per-person bounds included.
- **(v)** Ruling (j)'s README sentence is replaced; the rest of ruling (j) holds. From this amendment the README may be rewritten, by a docs PR of its own after the strategy rewrite (ruling t), to state the goal as the project's aim. It claims only what a published experiment has measured: EXP-001 or EXP-002, as under ruling (j), or now a later pre-registered one. It names EXP-001 and EXP-002 as not yet reported until they are.
- **(w)** §4's licence default applies, as the review's second ruling found. No commercial conversation is in progress, so everything outside the reserved seams moves from BUSL-1.1 to Apache-2.0. A licence PR of its own makes the change, the maintainer merges it, and until then every file keeps the licence it has. That PR settles:
  - **where the line runs**: by default the four [reserved seams](open-core-reserved-seams.md) are interfaces kept for Private code, which is never published ([RFC 0045 §C](rfcs/0045-open-core-extraction-policy.md#c-the-private-tier-reserved-seams-and-the-no-retraction-rule)), so every published file moves. The licence PR's RFC 0045 amendment confirms that default, or names each file that keeps BUSL-1.1 and why;
  - **provenance**, as RFC 0045's criterion 5 asks: every file is the copyright holder's alone or covered by a contributor agreement that grants relicensing rights (RFC 0045 §E). CONTRIBUTING's inbound clause, which ties a contribution to BUSL-1.1 and its Change Date, is not such an agreement. The bots' commits are checked too, Dependabot's bumps and the Copilot agent's fix ([#994](https://github.com/mkhomutov/Persatrix/pull/994)) among them;
  - **the released tags**: whether the grant reaches them, or each keeps BUSL-1.1 until its Change Date, when the LICENSE already turns it into Apache-2.0;
  - **what states the licence**: every live statement of it that `git grep -i -e busl -e "business source"` finds outside plans and reports, LICENSE and the README's License section among them. Plans and reports already written keep their wording as history;
  - **an RFC 0045 amendment** for its three tiers, the `MIT ← BUSL ← Private` import direction, the reserved-seams note's rule that an ambiguous new capability defaults to BUSL, and the wording of the no-retraction rule.

  The MIT tier stays as it is, and so do RFC 0046's and RFC 0047's plans for it. The no-retraction rule keeps its substance, that nothing published moves into the Private tier; the RFC 0045 amendment restates it without naming a licence, its memory-tier bullet included. The licence PR is not v0.4.0 scope and waits for no tag; it adds a CHANGELOG `[Unreleased]` entry, so the next release's notes say so.
- **(x)** §4's other default applies, as the review found. The workflow engine is frozen, because EXP-001 does not use it: no new workflow features (definitions, planning, scheduling, run state), wherever the code lives; most of it is in `internal/planner`, `scheduler`, `executor` and `state`. As with the Rust CLI, the packages are not deleted, and fixes with no issue behind them (dependency, security, CI) still land; a fix for a filed issue, such as removing ISSUE-0035's dead chat executor, boards only by ruling (q) or (u). If the accepted PR plan of a phase v0.4.0 boards (RFC 0009 Phase 4 or RFC 0028 Phase 2a) needs a workflow step paused for approval, that one change comes with the phase. The engine leaves the README's pitch in the README PR (ruling v). [RFC 0007](rfcs/0007-conditional-looped-workflow-control-flow.md) and RFC 0014 Phases 2–3, unslotted by ruling (m), board only with an amendment that also unfreezes the engine, which a goal audit may propose by ruling (u)'s route.
