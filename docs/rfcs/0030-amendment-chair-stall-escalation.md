# RFC 0030 Amendment — Chair Stall Escalation (a Minimal Layer 5 Slice, Brought Forward)

**Type**: amendment to [RFC 0030 §I](0030-multi-agent-conversation-governance.md#i-layer-5--moderator-role) (Layer 5 — moderator role), bringing one narrow slice forward from Phase 2 (v0.4.0) — the floor-control precedent (Layer 2.5, brought forward to v0.3.6 as a usability blocker)
**Status**: 📋 Proposed
**Author**: Maksim Khomutov
**Date**: 2026-06-11
**Target**: v0.3.8 (three implementation PRs after this doc: orchestrator stall detection + escalation dispatch; agent-side forced turn; MT + closeout)
**Trigger**: With the interaction-id producer live (PRs [#604](https://github.com/mkhomutov/Persatrix/pull/604)–[#607](https://github.com/mkhomutov/Persatrix/pull/607)), conversations have a working semantic terminator — [MT-CHANNEL-GOV-003](../manual-tests/MT-CHANNEL-GOV-003.md) passed live: a discussion that *flows* converges on votes. The remaining silent-death mode is the **stall**: an open-floor round where every Tier B `participant` honestly bids "nothing new to add" and the room goes quiet with the question **unresolved** — no synthesis stated, no votes cast, no close. Today that state just stands until idle rotation buries it (`trigger=idle`, ten minutes later, outcome unrecorded as a decision). The convergence review that motivated this whole workstream named the defect class precisely: *the bid asks "do I have something new to add?" and a zero-bid outcome terminates by silence — in any real meeting, a zero-bid round on an open question escalates to the chair, who synthesizes or calls on someone.* Silence and consensus remain indistinguishable until something is forced to tell them apart.
**Supersedes**: nothing — strictly additive. The full §I moderator (transcript-reading CONTINUE/WRAP_UP/TERMINATE every N turns, the trusted non-participant closer, bid-and-select) remains v0.4.0 and absorbs this slice when it lands (§E).

---

## Context — what the stall looks like after v0.3.8

1. A human (or agent) poses a question; a round of `participant` replies lands — each subsequent open-floor message runs the Tier B salience bid.
2. Round N: every candidate's bid scores below threshold (their points are made). Every floor turn passes silently. **Nobody votes** — voting means "we are done", and no persona judges an unresolved question done; the bid means "I have nothing to add", which is true for all of them simultaneously.
3. The interaction sits open. The Layer 4 quorum can never form (votes only ride publishes, and nobody publishes). Idle rotation closes it ten minutes later with no synthesis, and the interaction summary records a discussion that trailed off — not a decision.

The deterministic layers cannot fix this: every one of them answers "should this message be suppressed?", and the stall is a state with **no message to judge**. The fix needs the one thing the governance stack deliberately lacks below Layer 5 — something that *generates* a turn instead of gating one. This amendment adds the smallest such generator that composes with everything already landed.

## A. Decisions

- **CE1 — the stall signal is deterministic and orchestrator-side; no LLM detects it.** The floor round already observes the stall exactly: [`floorRound`](../../internal/channels/floor_control.go) grants every candidate a serialized turn and records each outcome (`floor_turn{outcome}`) — a round whose every turn ends `timeout` (zero `replied`) on a channel with an **open, tracked interaction** is the stall, observed for free at round end (a round with at least one granted turn — §C 1; and the signal reads *outcomes, not reasons*: an honest bid-pass and a crashed agent both end `timeout`, a conflation CE7's fail-open posture absorbs — escalating at a dead agent degrades to the same silence). No transcript reading, no new cost class — the full §I moderator's LLM judgement ("is this *productive*?") stays in v0.4.0; this slice answers only the cheaper question the round outcome already encodes ("did this produce *anything*?").
- **CE2 — the escalation target is an explicit per-channel knob: `escalation_chair_id`.** It cannot be inferred: the `chair` disposition does not survive persistence — the [canonicalization amendment](0030-amendment-disposition-vocabulary-canonicalization.md)'s encoding-vs-surface rule normalizes a declared `chair` to an `always` row whose `salience_gated`/`threshold` pair is indistinguishable from an operator-tuned `participant` ([`sqlite_schema.go`](../../internal/channels/sqlite_schema.go) CHECK admits only the legacy triple). Deliberately **not** §I's `moderator_agent_id`: that knob names a *non-participant trusted closer* with a `close_interaction` capability (a different trust model, reserved for v0.4.0); `escalation_chair_id` names a *participant member* who receives one extra speaking turn and closes nothing (CE4). Validation: must be a member of the channel; need not be declared `chair` (any participant can be the designated synthesizer), though the demo config pairs them. Absent (the default) → no escalation, the v0.3.8 status quo — opt-in like every governance knob.
- **CE3 — the escalation is ONE directed forced turn, dispatched at stall detection.** At the stalled round's end the router re-dispatches the round's stimulus to `escalation_chair_id` alone, with a `chair_escalation: true` marker on the `ChannelMessageEvent` (a first-class field — the event has no metadata map; the `cascade_depth` precedent). Receiver-side, the marker routes the event down the **directed lane**: the response gate admits it (the marker is orchestrator-authored, the same trust class as `floor_mentions`/`salience_gated`), and the Tier B bid is **skipped** — TB1's directed-address contract; the persona already bid "nothing new" on this stimulus, and re-running the bid would re-produce the very silence being escalated. The persona-side prompt frames the turn (a dedicated escalation section, rendered only when the marker is present): *the room has gone quiet with the question unresolved — either (a) state the synthesis as you see it and cast your end-of-discussion vote, or (b) name the member best placed to resolve what remains and ask them directly.* Outcome (a) publishes synthesis + vote — one quorum vote short of close, and the synthesis itself is the open-floor stimulus the others' bids re-judge ("do I agree / object?" is new content even when "do I have new arguments?" was not). Outcome (b) publishes a directed mention, which admits the named member through the existing `mentioned` lane.
- **CE4 — termination still flows through Layer 4 alone; TB5 is intact.** The chair **proposes, the quorum disposes**: an escalation turn can end in the chair's own vote, but closing still requires a second distinct member to concur within the W-turn window — exactly the landed machinery, no new close trigger, no `close_interaction` capability, no change to the "a chair cannot close a conversation" pin ([`chair_moderation.py`](../../agents/chair_moderation.py) stays inert — its CONTINUE-only seam is the v0.4.0 transcript-level judgement, which this slice deliberately does not make). The resolution **artifact** needs nothing new either: the chair's synthesis is a real message in history, and the close lands it in the interaction summary the surface already hands back (PRs [#606](https://github.com/mkhomutov/Persatrix/pull/606)/[#607](https://github.com/mkhomutov/Persatrix/pull/607)). A separate `PROPOSE_RESOLUTION` action is deferred (§OQ 2).
- **CE5 — at most one escalation per interaction.** The loop guard: if the chair's synthesis round *also* stalls (every bid passes on it, no second vote arrives), the interaction has had its escalation — the stall stands and idle rotation remains the net. Without this, a synthesis that draws no reaction re-triggers detection every round: chair speaks to silence, forever. Dedup state rides the interaction (a `chairEscalated` mark on the open-interaction entry; discarded with it — no new lifetime map). One escalation is the entire v0.3.8 ration; "escalate differently the second time" is moderator-grade judgement, v0.4.0's problem.
- **CE6 — the forced turn is one leased call billed to the interaction.** Layer 1 composes unchanged: the chair's turn (and the bids its synthesis triggers) ride the same `interaction_id` attribution the producer plan landed. A cost-exhausted interaction therefore cannot be escalated into more spend — the lease denial fails the forced turn closed, the same as any turn. No §I moderator sub-budget in this slice (one turn per interaction does not need its own envelope).
- **CE7 — fail-open, off the hot path.** Escalation is fire-and-forget at the floor round's tail: every degraded branch nets to the status quo (the stall stands; idle rotation buries it) — never an error on the publish path, never a blocked round. The orchestrator can only see its own half: an unset knob, a spent ration, and a dispatch failure each emit `chair_escalation{outcome=...}` (§C 1); a gate-suppressed or LLM-failed forced turn is **agent-side and invisible to the dispatcher** — it surfaces as the synthesis round's ordinary `floor_turn{outcome=timeout}`, not as a `chair_escalation` outcome. The marker is advisory agent-side too: an agent that does not recognise it (version skew — old agent, new orchestrator) sees an ordinary open-floor message and re-runs the very bid that produced the stall (§D — silence again, not a conversational decline); the wire field is additive (proto3 default false = no escalation, byte-identical for old producers).

## B. What does NOT change

- **The trust model.** No new principal, no new capability, no close path outside Layer 4's quorum. The chair gains exactly one thing: an extra *speaking* turn it would not otherwise have taken.
- **The deterministic layers.** Depth cap, cost ceiling, reply budget, end-vote quorum, idle rotation: untouched. The escalation turn and its consequences are ordinary governed traffic under all of them (the chair's forced turn consumes its Layer 2 reply allowance like any publish — a budget-exhausted chair fails CE7-open).
- **Tier B.** The bid still governs every open-floor admit, including the synthesis round the escalation produces. The escalation does not lower anyone's threshold; it changes the *question* the room faces (from the stalled stimulus to a concrete synthesis), which is what makes fresh bids meaningful.
- **The inert `chair_moderation.py` seam and TB5.** Still CONTINUE-only, still uncalled. The v0.4.0 moderator replaces the seam body *and* this amendment's stall heuristic together (§E).

## C. Mechanism (implementation sketch — PR plan follows this doc)

1. **Go (PR 2 of the workstream).** `floorRound` aggregates the per-turn outcomes it already records into a round outcome and exposes it to its caller (today it records only per-turn `floor_turn{outcome}` and returns nothing). Stall detection at round end is `zero replied ∧ ≥1 granted turn ∧ open tracked interaction` — the granted-turn floor excludes the vacuously silent round (an empty candidate list is not a stall; nobody was asked anything). Every **detected** stall emits `channel.conversation.chair_escalation{channel_type, outcome}` with its disposition — `no_chair` (knob unset), `already_escalated` (CE5 mark present), `dispatch_error`, or `dispatched` — so the unreachable-outcome trap is avoided: the disposition checks happen after detection, not as preconditions of the metric. On `dispatched`, mark and send the forced turn: the stimulus message, recipient = the chair member's envelope, `chair_escalation` marker, and a **fresh event id** — the agent-side conversation window dedups by message id ([`conversation_window.py`](../../agents/persona_runtime/conversation_window.py)), so redelivering the stalled message under its original id is not a reliable turn. Config: `escalation_chair_id` (channels.yaml per-channel + schema + member-existence validation at load). The mark lives on the resolver's open-interaction entry and dies with it (CE5).
2. **Python (PR 3).** Wire lift of the marker (`channel_wire_metadata`), gate admit for the marked dispatch (directed lane; defence-in-depth: honour the marker only on the typed field, the `floor_mentions_resolved` posture), Tier B bypass (TB1), the escalation prompt section (rendered on the marker, the `end-interaction-vote` snippet's sibling), drift pins for the marker literal.
3. **MT + closeout (PR 4).** The stall arc live: closable question → discussion → engineered stall (a question whose follow-up nobody has) → chair synthesis + vote → second member concurs → `trigger=end_votes` close with the synthesis in the summary. Deterministic acceptance extends [`interaction_convergence_test.go`](../../internal/channels/interaction_convergence_test.go)'s arc family.

## D. Mixed-version analysis

- **New orchestrator + old agent**: the marker field is unknown to the old agent — and nothing *else* about the dispatch is directed. The landed gate derives directedness from message **content** (`mentions`, the DM channel prefix — [`response_gate.py`](../../agents/response_gate.py)), never from delivery targeting, so a single-recipient re-dispatch of the stalled stimulus is indistinguishable from the open-floor original. The old agent therefore re-runs the very Tier B bid that produced the stall (open-floor `policy_always` admit → bid → silence again), or — if the stalled stimulus named someone else — is suppressed `directed_elsewhere` before any LLM runs. Either way the round stays silent and the stall stands, byte-identical to the v0.3.8 status quo; what does **not** happen is a conversational decline — suppression is upstream of the quality turn. Degraded but safe (CE7); no breakage. Both lanes are already pinned in [`test_response_gate_relevance.py`](../../tests/unit/python/test_response_gate_relevance.py).
- **Old orchestrator + new agent**: the marker never arrives; the escalation prompt section never renders; the stall stands as in v0.3.8. No breakage.
- **No DB change.** The dedup mark is in-memory resolver state (IP5 restart semantics apply: a restart forgets the escalation, and the fresh interaction gets a fresh ration).

## E. Relationship to the v0.4.0 moderator (§I)

This slice is the floor-control move replayed at Layer 5: bring forward the *deterministic* fragment that unblocks the product story, leave the LLM-judgement fragment where it is. The full moderator subsumes it cleanly: bid-and-select replaces the zero-bid heuristic (a moderator that picks speakers makes "nobody bid" nearly unreachable), `moderator_agent_id` supersedes `escalation_chair_id` (a config-load warning will steer migration), and the MODERATE_INTERACTION event replaces the escalation marker. Nothing in this amendment grows a surface the moderator must work around — the knob, the marker, and the mark are all deletable.

## Open questions

1. **Should the escalation also fire on a quorum-pending stall** (one vote cast, then a zero-replied round)? Resolved here: **yes by construction** — the trigger is round-shaped, not vote-aware; a silent round after a lone vote is exactly a stalled round. Note the landed window mechanics make this *safe*, not urgent: W counts **turns** — publishes carrying the `interaction_id` ([`end_vote.go`](../../internal/channels/end_vote.go)'s per-interaction counter) — so a zero-replied round advances zero turns and the lone vote **cannot go stale during the silence**; what eventually kills it is idle rotation closing the whole interaction, not window expiry. The escalation therefore always lands while the pending vote is live: the chair's synthesis-and-vote is one turn later (distance 1 < W), and even a synthesis that draws a separate concurrence closes at distance W−1 ([`TestEndVote_PendingVoteSurvivesSilenceAndEscalationTurn`](../../internal/channels/end_vote_test.go) pins exactly this arc). Pinned in the PR 2 matrix.
2. **`PROPOSE_RESOLUTION` as a first-class action.** Deferred: the chair's synthesis message + the interaction summary already carry the artifact, and a typed resolution record belongs with RFC 0028's `DecisionRecord` consolidation (v0.4.0). Revisit if MT evidence shows the free-text synthesis is too lossy to mine.
3. **Multiple chairs / rotation.** One knob, one target, in this slice. A channel wanting rotating facilitation is a Layer 6 conversation-type concern.
4. **Should the escalation prompt let the chair decline?** Yes — "if the question genuinely cannot be advanced by this room, say so and vote" is a valid synthesis; what it may not do is stay silent (the forced turn exists because silence is the failure mode). The prompt half is calibratable; the mechanism does not care which of (a)/(b) the chair picks.
